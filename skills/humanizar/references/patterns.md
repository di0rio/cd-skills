# Catalog of AI tics

One occurrence doesn't condemn a text. The problem is piling up: three or four of these in a paragraph and the reader knows. When revising, count; when writing, avoid them by default.

## Contents
1. English words and phrases
2. Portuguese (PT-BR) words and phrases
3. Sentence constructions (both languages)
4. Text structure
5. Formatting and punctuation
6. Tics that survive a first revision

## 1. English words and phrases

**Openings**: "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving landscape of", "Let's dive in", "Picture this:", "Have you ever wondered".

**Verbs**: delve, leverage, utilize, foster, harness, unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, streamline, navigate (abstract), embark, showcase, underscore, highlight, bolster, spearhead.

**Adjectives/nouns**: pivotal, crucial, vital, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, groundbreaking, transformative, holistic, comprehensive, nuanced, multifaceted, meticulous, tapestry, realm, landscape, testament, journey, synergy, paradigm.

**Filler**: "It's worth noting that", "It's important to remember", "Notably,", "Moreover,", "Furthermore,", "Additionally,", "Ultimately,", "In essence,", "At its core,".

**Closings**: "In conclusion,", "Overall,", "In summary,", "The future is bright", "Only time will tell", "...and that's what truly matters."

**Sycophancy/meta** (when the text is a reply): "Great question!", "Certainly!", "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if you'd like...".

**LinkedIn tone**: "I'm thrilled to announce...", "Humbled and grateful", "What a journey!", "🚀", "Agree?" at the end, "Lessons learned:" followed by 3-5 bullets with emoji.

## 2. Portuguese (PT-BR) words and phrases

**Generic openings**: "Em um mundo cada vez mais (conectado/digital/competitivo)...", "No cenário atual...", "Nos dias de hoje...", "Na era digital...", "Você já parou para pensar...?", "Vamos mergulhar em...", "Vamos explorar...".

**Too many connectives** (one here and there is normal; one per sentence is a tic): "Além disso,", "Ademais,", "Outrossim,", "Nesse sentido,", "Nesse contexto,", "Dessa forma,", "Desse modo,", "Vale ressaltar que", "Cabe destacar que", "É importante salientar que", "É fundamental compreender que".

**Summary closings**: "Em suma,", "Em síntese,", "Diante do exposto,", "Portanto, conclui-se que", "Fica evidente que...", "Torna-se claro que...", "Afinal, ..." as a final punchline.

**Inflated vocabulary** (and the plain alternative)
| Inflated | Plain |
|---|---|
| potencializar, alavancar | aumentar, melhorar, usar |
| robusto | sólido, confiável (or say what) |
| fundamental, crucial, essencial, de suma importância | importante (or cut) |
| desempenha um papel fundamental | ajuda, serve para |
| jornada | caminho, processo (or cut) |
| mergulhar, explorar a fundo | ver, estudar |
| desbloquear, destravar (potencial) | permitir, abrir |
| transformador, revolucionário, disruptivo | say what changed |
| ecossistema, panorama, cenário | área, mercado, contexto (or cut) |
| impulsionar | aumentar, ajudar |
| otimizar | melhorar, acelerar (say how much) |
| evidenciar, salientar | mostrar |
| proporcionar | dar, trazer |
| garantir | only when there's a real guarantee |

**LinkedIn tone**: "Estou muito feliz/animado em compartilhar que...", "Gratidão 🙏", "Humildemente...", "Que jornada!", "Bora!", "Fica a dica!", "Concorda?" at the end.

## 3. Sentence constructions

- **"It's not just X, it's Y" / "Não é X, é Y" / "More than X, it's Y".** A made-up contrast to sound deep. Say Y directly.
- **Rule of three.** "fast, efficient and scalable", "learn, grow and evolve". AI loves trios. Use two or four, or one, depending on the content.
- **Rhetorical question followed by the answer.** "The result? A system 3x faster." Once per text is fine. Repeated, it's a formula.
- **Punchline with a colon.** "The truth is simple: ..." / "Here's the thing: ...".
- **Dangling participles (EN).** "..., ensuring that...", "..., making it ideal for...". Break them into sentences.
- **Cascading gerunds (PT).** "...permitindo que os usuários acessem, garantindo segurança e proporcionando uma experiência...". Same problem.
- **Vague attribution.** "Experts say", "studies show", "many believe". Name who, or cut.
- **Double hedge.** "may potentially", "pode potencialmente". Pick one.
- **Empty praise of the topic.** "a fascinating topic", "um tema extremamente relevante".
- **Personified abstractions.** "Data tells a story", "technology invites us to reflect".

## 4. Text structure

- **Intro that announces, body that lists, conclusion that repeats the intro.** Cut the intro and conclusion if they add nothing.
- **Same-size paragraphs**, each with a topic sentence, two supporting sentences and a transition.
- **Every section ends with a moral.** "And that makes all the difference."
- **A list where prose would fit.** Three half-line bullets that should be one sentence.
- **Headings for short text.** A 150-word post doesn't need "## Introduction".
- **Fake balance.** "On the one hand... on the other hand..." when the author has a clear opinion.
- **Uniform rhythm.** Every sentence between 15 and 25 words. Real people vary.

## 5. Formatting and punctuation

- **Too many em dashes.** The strongest tell in Portuguese, where they're rare in informal writing. Use a comma, a period, parentheses or a colon. English tolerates them a bit more.
- **Bold everywhere.** One or two highlights per text, at most.
- **Emoji as bullet markers** (✅ 🚀 💡 🔥 👉).
- **Title Case in Portuguese headings** ("Como Melhorar Sua Produtividade"). In Portuguese, only the first letter.
- **Curly quotes mixed with straight ones**, a single-character ellipsis (…) in text that is otherwise typed "by hand". Small detail, but it adds up.
- **A block of hashtags** at the end of a post.

## 6. Tics that survive a first revision

Text that has already gone through "remove the inflated words" often keeps these. They're subtler and tend to show up precisely in the rewrite.

- **Abstract range "from X to Y".** "From the database and API all the way to the last screen state", "from idea to deploy", "end to end", "do banco à tela". Sounds broad and shows nothing. Replace with a situation: "if a screen needs a new route, I build it".
- **Slogan at the end.** A short, rounded sentence that sums up the previous one as a motto: "nothing gets in until I understand it", "code I can't explain doesn't get in", "the best code is the code never written". It works once on a landing page; in a bio or post it reads like a poster. Test: would it fit on a mug? Cut it.
- **Colon plus a list of three.** "I like the part nobody sees: the loading, the error, the speed." The trio after a colon is the tic that most often slips through, because each item looks concrete. Use two items, one well-chosen item, or prose.
- **Clever image.** "The part that never shows up in a screenshot", "the code nobody sees", "where the magic happens". An image that's the person's own is voice; an image any developer would use is decoration. If the person wouldn't talk like that, remove it. If the person doesn't even understand it, definitely remove it.
- **Abstraction instead of a scene.** "Screen states", "user experience", "code quality" on their own show nothing. Say what happens: "the table doesn't flicker when you filter".
- **Symmetric pairs "no X, no Y".** "No fluff, no jargon." "Sem enrolação, sem jargão." Ad rhythm.
- **Noun-phrase lists.** "Focus on performance, accessibility and best practices." Three abstract nouns with no verb. Say what you do with them, or cut.
- **Echo of the reference.** In inspire mode: same paragraph structure, same order of ideas, an expression translated over ("software people spend their whole workday in" turning into "pra quem passa o expediente nela"). Anyone who knows the reference notices.
- **Dramatic "but".** "I use AI, but I only commit what I understand." A "but" that exists to sound firm, not because there's a real opposition. Sometimes it's true; often it's enough to state the second part.
